# 3D landing page: story variants

Six alternate concept stories for the 3D landing page, beside the first one,
[Cut to Measure](3d-landing-story.md) (variant A). Every variant has to explain the same five things:

1. **What it is:** a CRM tailored to one industry and one business, owned by that business.
2. **Features:** pipelines, tasks, quotes and invoices, bookings, client portal, documents, support desk,
   inbox and calendar, automations, reports.
3. **Use cases:** nine industry templates, from clinics to law firms to coworking.
4. **Dynamicity:** one product that reshapes itself through vocabulary, stages, fields, objects and modules,
   without a fork or a custom build.
5. **AI:** the assistant in daily work (with its safety rules), and AI setup mode, which builds the CRM
   for the business.

Each variant takes a different route through them. All claims stay true to the product today.

---

## Variant B: The Living Blueprint

**One line.** _Every business has a plan. We build the CRM from yours._

**The world.** An architect's drafting table in the dark. White and gold lines on deep blue-green paper that
rise into 3D buildings. It fits because the product's industry templates are literally called blueprints.

**Hero.** An empty sheet of drafting paper with a faint grid. A gold line draws itself across it:
_Every business has a plan._ The line folds up into a wireframe building:
_Your CRM should be built from it._

| Act | Scene                                                                                                                                                                | What it proves                                   |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | **The brief.** An AI architect interviews the owner. Each answer becomes a line on the plan: rooms (records), corridors (stages), doors (roles).                     | AI setup interview and website read              |
| 2   | **The drawing.** The plan is shown in full, every line labelled. The owner crosses one out, moves another, and stamps it _Approved_.                                 | AI proposes, admin confirms                      |
| 3   | **The build.** The plan rises into a 3D building in seconds. The signage on the doors reads _Patients_, _Treatment plans_. The facade takes the brand colors.        | Vocabulary, stages, fields, branding             |
| 4   | **The city.** The camera pulls up: nine buildings on one shared foundation, one per industry, each different above ground.                                           | Nine templates, one codebase                     |
| 5   | **The rooms.** Fly through the building: a front desk (bookings), a vault (invoices), a lobby window clients look through (portal), a post room (inbox).             | Modules                                          |
| 6   | **The people.** Tiny lit figures move through the corridors all day. Doors open on their own when a quote is accepted.                                               | Daily work and automations                       |
| 7   | **The building manager.** The AI walks the halls with a master key that only opens the doors the person asking can open. Every door it opens is written in a ledger. | Assistant, permissions, Confirm, audit log       |
| 8   | **The deeds.** The foundation is shown to sit on the owner's own land, with their name on the deed.                                                                  | Own instance and database, white-label, security |
| 9   | **Extensions.** A new wing is drawn and built a year later, without knocking anything down.                                                                          | Objects, modules, integrations                   |

**End.** _Bring us your plan._ **Book a walkthrough.**

**Strengths.** Uses the product's own word (blueprint), so sales and docs line up. The city is a strong
"one product, many industries" image. **Risk.** Architecture is a common SaaS metaphor; the look must be
distinctive to avoid feeling like a construction-software site.

## Variant C: Say It, See It Built

**One line.** _Describe your business. Watch your CRM assemble itself._

**The world.** The whole page is a conversation with the AI. Every message the visitor scrolls past makes
3D pieces fly in and snap together beside it, like a model kit assembling in mid-air. The AI is the main
character from the first second.

**Hero.** A blinking cursor on a dark stage, then a message types itself:
_"We're a dental clinic with three chairs and nine staff."_ Nothing exists yet. The reply comes:
_"Let's build your CRM. Tell me how a patient finds you."_

| Act | Message on screen                                       | What builds itself in 3D                                                                | What it proves                       |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| 1   | _"Here's our website."_                                 | The site is scanned once by a beam of light; logo, colors and services drop into a tray | Website read, branding               |
| 2   | _"Enquiry, consult, plan, treatment, recall."_          | Five stage blocks slide into a pipeline rail                                            | Stages in your words                 |
| 3   | _"We call them patients."_                              | Every label in the model flips like a split-flap board: Contacts → Patients             | Vocabulary everywhere                |
| 4   | _"Reception books, dentists treat, the manager bills."_ | Three role keys are cut, each opening different doors                                   | Roles and record access              |
| 5   | The AI: _"Here's what I'll set up."_                    | The full model holds still, every part labelled. A **Confirm** button glows             | Nothing changes without confirmation |
| 6   | _"Import this spreadsheet."_                            | Rows pour in, matched to fields by threads; 31 rows flagged red before saving           | Spreadsheet mapping, check first     |
| 7   | _"We also send quotes, take bookings, chase forms."_    | Module pieces snap on: quotes, invoices, bookings, portal, documents, support, calendar | Modules                              |
| 8   | Next morning: _"Which plans are stuck in Consult?"_     | Four cards lift out of the model; drafts of follow-up emails appear                     | The assistant in daily work          |
| 9   | Swap the first message: _"We're a law firm."_           | The whole model melts down and reassembles as matters, letters and a client portal      | Nine industries, one product         |

**The safety moment.** Between acts 7 and 8 the AI stops and shows its four rules as physical locks on the
model: it sees what you see, it asks first, it logs everything, you choose the provider.

**End.** _Your CRM is one conversation away._ **Try it with your business** (book a demo) or **Open the live demo**.

**Strengths.** The clearest possible story for "AI builds it for the business". Interactive variant: the
visitor picks their industry in act 0 and the whole page re-runs for them. **Risk.** Leans so hard on AI
that the human setup help and data ownership can feel secondary; those need a strong act of their own.

## Variant D: One Enquiry's Journey

**One line.** _Follow one customer through a business that never drops the ball._

**The world.** A single glowing point of light (one person's enquiry) travels through a 3D miniature of the
business. The camera follows it like a drone shot. It's a story about outcomes first, and the machinery is
revealed afterwards.

**Hero.** Night. A phone lights up in a city of small dark buildings. A point of light leaves it:
_Every customer starts as one message._ _Most businesses lose some along the way._

| Act | Where the light goes                                                                                                                        | What it proves                        |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Into the inbox. It's caught, checked against existing records (no duplicate) and lands as a card                                            | Inbox, web forms, duplicate rules     |
| 2   | Across the pipeline: _Enquiry → Consult_. A booking snaps into a free slot; a clashing one bounces off                                      | Pipelines, bookings                   |
| 3   | A quote leaves the building, is accepted on a phone in the portal, and comes back as an invoice                                             | Quotes, portal, invoices, e-signature |
| 4   | Accepting fires a chain of gold sparks: stage moved, task created, welcome email sent, follow-up in 7 days                                  | Automations, delayed and scheduled    |
| 5   | A question arrives later; it becomes a ticket with a response target, answered in the portal                                                | Support desk                          |
| 6   | Six months on, a recall reminder fires on its own and the light returns                                                                     | Repeating tasks, reminders            |
| 7   | **Rewind.** The camera flies backwards through everything at speed. _Who built all this?_                                                   | Transition                            |
| 8   | The rewind lands on day one: an empty city and an AI asking the owner questions. Each answer raises a building, renames a sign, lays a road | AI setup mode, proposal, Confirm      |
| 9   | The same city, re-skinned for a law firm, then a real estate agency, as the light follows a different customer                              | Industries and dynamicity             |
| 10  | The assistant hovers over the city answering questions; its four rules glow on the city walls                                               | Assistant and guardrails              |
| 11  | The city sits inside a sealed glass dome with the owner's name on it                                                                        | Ownership, security, white-label      |

**End.** _Never lose another enquiry._ **Book a demo.**

**Strengths.** Speaks the business owner's language (lost leads, slow quotes, forgotten follow-ups) before
any feature list. The rewind is a memorable twist. **Risk.** The longest scroll; the AI-builds-it message
arrives late, so act 8 has to be the visual high point.

## Variant E: The Shape-Shifter

**One line.** _One CRM. Every shape._

**The world.** Abstract and sculptural. A single object made of liquid metal and glass floats in a dark
room. It is the product. As you scroll, it reshapes itself, and words orbit it like rings around a planet.
Closest to an award-site or product-launch feel.

**Hero.** The object hangs still, a plain sphere. Words circle it slowly: _Contacts. Deals. Activities._
_Generic software has one shape._ The sphere ripples. _Yours shouldn't._

| Act | How the object changes                                                                                                                      | What it proves                   |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 1   | A gold pulse (the AI) runs over the surface, "listening". Questions orbit; answers condense into small droplets on the surface              | AI interview                     |
| 2   | The droplets form a shape on the surface: a preview, outlined, not yet solid. A ring of light reads _Confirm_; when crossed, it solidifies  | Proposal and Confirm             |
| 3   | The orbiting words morph letter by letter: _Deals → Treatment plans → Listings → Matters → Placements → Enrolments_                         | Vocabulary across industries     |
| 4   | The object splits into nine forms, one per industry, then flows back into one                                                               | Nine templates, one codebase     |
| 5   | Modules grow out of it as smaller satellites, each opening into a real product screen as it passes the camera                               | Modules                          |
| 6   | It starts pulsing with life: small lights travel its surface like traffic (tasks done, quotes accepted, emails sent)                        | Daily work, automations, reports |
| 7   | The gold pulse returns, now answering questions. Four rings lock around it: _sees what you see, asks first, logs everything, your provider_ | Assistant and guardrails         |
| 8   | The object is placed inside a case with the owner's name engraved on it                                                                     | Ownership, white-label, security |

**End.** _Shape it to your business._ **Book a demo.**

**Strengths.** The most visually striking and the most "3D"; very strong for dynamicity. Works well as a
hero for paid campaigns. **Risk.** Abstract: a busy clinic owner may not connect a liquid sphere to their
appointments. Product screens must appear early and often.

## Variant F: Nine Rooms, One House

**One line.** _Walk into your industry. It's already set up for you._

**The world.** A single house with nine doors around a central hall. Each door opens onto a different
business, fully furnished in 3D: a dental surgery, an estate agency window, a law office, a broker's desk,
a building site, a classroom, a recruitment floor, a visa office, a coworking space. Use case first,
features second.

**Hero.** A round hall, softly lit, nine doors with nameplates. _Which one is yours?_ The visitor picks a
door, or scrolls and the camera picks one for them.

| Act | Scene                                                                                                                                                             | What it proves                          |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Through the chosen door: the room for that industry. Objects on the desk are the CRM: a pipeline board on the wall, a booking book, a filing cabinet of documents | Industry use case, vocabulary           |
| 2   | Touch any object and it opens into its real product screen with that industry's words                                                                             | Features, in context                    |
| 3   | A gold figure (the assistant) sits at the desk. Ask it something and it answers from the room's records, drafts the email, waits for Confirm                      | Assistant and guardrails                |
| 4   | Back in the hall, the visitor walks to a tenth door with no nameplate: _Not on the list?_                                                                         | Bridge to AI setup                      |
| 5   | Behind it, an empty room. The assistant interviews you and furnishes it piece by piece: names, stages, fields, roles, your logo on the wall                       | AI setup mode, proposal, branding       |
| 6   | Boxes of old files are carried in and unpacked into the cabinets, sorted and checked first                                                                        | Spreadsheet import with checks          |
| 7   | Pull back: all ten rooms sit on the same foundations, wired to one system. The house is labelled with the owner's name                                            | One codebase, own instance, white-label |
| 8   | An extension is added to the house later                                                                                                                          | Objects, modules, integrations          |

**End.** _Your room is ready._ **Book a demo of your industry**, or **Open the live demo**.

**Strengths.** Every visitor sees their own industry within five seconds. Reuses the nine industry pages'
content directly (`src/data/industries.ts`). The empty tenth room is a clean way to introduce AI setup.
**Risk.** Ten furnished 3D rooms is the heaviest build; rooms could be stylised dioramas instead of
detailed interiors.

## Variant G: The Constellation

**One line.** _Your business is a network of relationships. See it, then run it._

**The world.** Deep space. Every record is a star: people, companies, deals, bookings, invoices. Lines
between them are relationships. The AI is a navigator that draws the map and answers questions about it.

**Hero.** A sky of scattered, unconnected points. _Your customers are everywhere: inboxes, spreadsheets,
calendars, sticky notes._ The points pull together into constellations. _Bespoq brings them into one sky._

| Act | Scene                                                                                                                            | What it proves                   |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 1   | Spreadsheets and inboxes dissolve into stars. Duplicates (two stars for one person) merge into one brighter star                 | Import, inbox, duplicate merge   |
| 2   | The navigator asks how the business works, then names the constellations in the owner's words: _Patients_, _Treatment plans_     | AI setup, vocabulary             |
| 3   | The proposed map is shown dotted; on Confirm, the lines turn solid                                                               | Proposal and Confirm             |
| 4   | The stars drift into orbits along the pipeline: each orbit a stage, each star moving outward as work progresses                  | Pipelines, boards                |
| 5   | New satellites launch from the main body: quotes, invoices, bookings, portal, documents, support                                 | Modules                          |
| 6   | Ask the navigator _"Who haven't we spoken to in 90 days?"_; those stars light up. _"Draft a note to each."_ It waits for Confirm | Assistant, reports, guardrails   |
| 7   | Zoom out: nine galaxies, one per industry, made of the same kind of stars                                                        | Nine templates, one codebase     |
| 8   | The galaxy sits inside a sphere with the owner's name on it; nothing leaves without their permission                             | Ownership, security, white-label |

**End.** _Bring your whole business into view._ **Book a demo.**

**Strengths.** Beautiful in WebGL and cheap to render (points and lines). Strong for "relationships" and
for reports and the assistant. **Risk.** Space is a very common tech metaphor and drifts away from the
existing brand (tailoring, dark green, gold).

## Recommendation

**A (Cut to Measure) with C's interaction blended in.** Keep the tailoring world, which fits the name and
the site's look, and replace acts 1–2 with C's "say it, see it built" conversation: the visitor picks
their industry at the start and the measuring, cutting and word-morphing play out for that industry. That
gives the strongest AI story without leaving the brand.

Other combinations that work: **F's door picker as the hero of A** (choose your industry, then the
tailoring story runs in that industry's words), or **D's rewind** as the transition between the "day in
the life" act and the AI setup act in any variant.
