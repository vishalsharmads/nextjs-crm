# Cut to Measure: the story behind a 3D landing page for Bespoq

A concept story for an alternate, scroll-driven 3D landing page. It explains what Bespoq is, what it does,
who it serves, how it adapts to each business, and how AI helps build it. It sets the narrative and the
scenes; visuals, technology and copy length are for the design step.

---

## The idea in one line

**Every business is a different shape. Most CRMs come in one size.** Bespoq is the CRM made the way a tailor
makes a suit: measured, cut, stitched and fitted to one business, with AI holding the tape measure.

## The metaphor

The site already lives on a tailor's cutting mat after dark: deep green, a faint grid, gold tape. The 3D
page takes that literally and turns it into a world you scroll through.

| In the story           | In the product                                                              |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The bolt of cloth      | The core CRM: contacts, organizations, leads, deals, activities             |
| The tape measure       | The AI setup interview: it learns how the business works                    |
| The paper pattern      | A blueprint: industry vocabulary, stages, fields, roles, sample data        |
| The cut                | Configuration: your words, your pipeline, your fields                       |
| The panels stitched on | Modules: quotes, invoices, bookings, portal, documents, tickets             |
| The gold thread        | The AI assistant, running through every part of the work                    |
| The fitting            | Going live: the team and their clients wearing it every day                 |
| The label sewn inside  | White-label: your name, logo, colors and domain, never ours                 |
| The alterations        | It keeps changing with you: new stages, objects, modules, without a rebuild |

One rule keeps the metaphor honest: **every scene ends on a real screen of the product.** The fabric is the
poetry, the product UI is the proof.

## The cast

To make it concrete, the story follows one business through the whole journey:

**Harbour Dental**, a clinic with three chairs and nine staff. Today their enquiries live in a shared inbox,
treatment plans in Word files, bookings in a separate calendar, and reminders on sticky notes. The owner,
Dr. Anika Rao, has tried a big general-purpose CRM. It talked about "Deals" and "Opportunities", needed a
consultant, and charged per seat. Her team stopped opening it after a month.

Halfway through, the story briefly re-cuts the same cloth for two other businesses, to show that the shape
changes while the cloth stays the same.

---

## Act 0: The bolt (hero)

**Scene.** Darkness, then a single bolt of deep-green cloth, lit from one side, slowly unrolling across the
screen toward the viewer. A faint grid is woven into it. It is endless and perfectly uniform.

**Words.** _Every business is a different shape._ Then, as the cloth keeps rolling, plain and identical:
_Most CRMs come in one size._

**Turn.** A gold tape measure snaps out of the dark and lies across the cloth.
_Bespoq is cut to measure._

**Action.** Two buttons: _Book a fitting_ and _Scroll to see how it's made_.

**What it proves.** The positioning: not a generic platform, not a months-long custom build, but a CRM
tailored to one industry and one business.

## Act 1: The measure (AI listens)

**Scene.** The tape measure begins to move on its own, wrapping around shapes that rise out of the cloth:
a reception desk, three dental chairs, a phone, a stack of paper forms. Each time it wraps something, a
small gold label appears with a measurement written in plain words.

**Story.** Anika doesn't fill in a settings form. She opens setup mode and the assistant asks her about the
clinic, the way a tailor asks how you'll wear the suit:

> "What do you call the people you help?" _Patients._
> "What happens between their first call and the last visit?" _Enquiry, consult, treatment plan, in
> treatment, recall._
> "Who does what?" _Reception books, practitioners treat, the practice manager bills._

She pastes the clinic's website. The assistant reads it once and picks up the services, the tone, the logo
and the brand colors.

**Measurements appear on screen as gold tags:** _Patients, not contacts._ _Five stages._ _Three roles._
_Treatment plans with line items._ _Consult rooms that can't be double-booked._ _Recall in six months._

**What it proves.** AI setup mode: an interview, a website read once, and a written brief of how the
business really works. It's a conversation, not a configuration screen.

## Act 2: The pattern and the cut (AI proposes, the admin decides)

**Scene.** The measurements fly up and become a paper pattern, drawn in gold lines over the cloth: pieces
labelled _Patients_, _Treatment plans_, _Consults_, _Recalls_. A pair of shears hangs above, waiting.

**Story.** The assistant turns the brief into a proposal: the names to use, the fields to add, the pipeline
stages, the roles and what each one can see, and the modules to switch on. Every line is shown before
anything happens. Anika removes one field, renames a stage, and presses **Confirm**.

The shears come down. The cloth is cut along the pattern, one clean pass.

**The word morph.** As the cut lands, the interface under the cloth changes its words in place:
_Contacts_ becomes **Patients**. _Deals_ becomes **Treatment plans**. _Won_ becomes **Completed**. The
pipeline rebuilds itself with her five stages. Her logo and colors flow into the header.

**Then her old spreadsheet.** A spreadsheet of 2,400 patients drops onto the mat. Gold threads run from its
columns to the new fields: _Mobile_ to phone, _DOB_ to date of birth, _Last visit_ to a date field. A dry run
checks every row first and flags the 31 that need attention, before anything is saved.

**What it proves.** The AI builds, but the admin decides. Proposals, Confirm, branding, spreadsheet mapping
and a check before import. Days of setup become an afternoon.

## Act 3: Same cloth, a different cut (dynamicity)

**Scene.** The camera pulls back. Next to Harbour Dental's garment, two more bolts unroll from the same
cloth and are cut by the same shears into different shapes.

- A **real estate agency**: _Deals_ becomes **Listings**, stages run _Appraisal → Listed → Under offer →
  Sold → Settled_, and bookings become open homes with buyer check-in.
- A **law firm**: _Deals_ becomes **Matters**, documents become engagement letters signed online, and
  clients upload evidence through their portal.

Then a ring of nine patterns, one per industry: real estate, healthcare clinics, law firms, financial
advisers and mortgage brokers, construction and trades, education and training, recruitment and staffing,
immigration and visa consultancies, coworking. The visitor can turn the ring and watch one interface re-cut
itself for each.

**Words.** _One product. One codebase. Your words, your stages, your fields._ Underneath, smaller:
_Not a fork, not a custom build. You still get every update._

**What it proves.** Blueprints, vocabulary everywhere, custom fields, and up to 20 objects an admin can
define (a clinic adds _Lab orders_; a coworking space adds _Lockers_) without writing code.

## Act 4: The stitching (modules)

**Scene.** Back to Harbour Dental. Panels of cloth float in and stitch themselves to the garment, each seam
running in gold. Each panel, as it locks in, turns into a small live window of the product.

1. **Quotes and invoices.** A treatment plan quote with line items. The patient accepts online, it becomes an
   invoice in one click, and payments are recorded as they arrive.
2. **Bookings.** A day board of three chairs. A booking slides into a taken slot and bounces back; it won't
   double-book.
3. **Client portal.** The patient signs in with an email link, signs the consent form, uploads an X-ray and
   sees their invoice.
4. **Documents and e-signature.** A letter generated from a template, signed online.
5. **Support desk.** A patient question with a response target and a reply they see in their portal.
6. **Scheduling links and calendar.** A practitioner's booking link, synced with the calendar they already use.
7. **Inbox.** Two-way email inside the record, so every conversation with the patient is in one place.

**Words.** _Switch on what you need. Leave out what you don't._

**What it proves.** The module catalog: one system instead of five tools held together with copy and paste.

## Act 5: The fitting (a day in the life)

**Scene.** The finished garment turns slowly and becomes the clinic itself, built from the same cloth, seen
in miniature from above as a day passes. The light moves from morning to evening.

- **8:00.** Reception opens _My work_: today's tasks, grouped by what's due. Three recalls, two forms to chase.
- **9:30.** A web enquiry arrives. A card drops into _Enquiry_ on the board, already assigned.
- **11:00.** The consult is done. The card moves to _Treatment plan_; the quote goes out.
- **14:00.** The patient accepts in the portal. An automation runs in gold, link by link: the stage moves,
  a task is created for the practice manager, and a welcome email is sent.
- **17:30.** A recall is scheduled to fire in six months. A comment with an @mention pings a colleague's
  phone.
- **Evening.** The dashboard settles: chairs booked, plans accepted, revenue by practitioner.

**Words.** _Built around how your team actually works, so they keep using it._

**What it proves.** Pipelines and boards, tasks with checklists, repeating tasks and reminders, comments,
automations that can wait or run on a schedule, reports and dashboards, and an installable app with
notifications.

## Act 6: The gold thread (AI, every day)

**Scene.** Zoom in on a single gold thread. It turns out it has been running through every scene so far:
through the measurements, the pattern, the seams and the day. Now it comes to the front.

**Story.** Anika types into the assistant beside the page:

> "Which treatment plans have been stuck in Consult for more than two weeks?"

The thread pulls four cards out of the board and lines them up.

> "Draft a friendly follow-up to each."

Four drafts appear, written in the clinic's tone.

> "Add a Recall stage after Completed."

The thread stops at a gate. A card shows the exact change. **Nothing happens until she presses Confirm.**
She does, and the pipeline grows a stage.

**The gates.** The thread passes through four visible gates that it can never go around:

- **It sees only what you can see.** A receptionist's assistant can't see what a receptionist can't.
- **It asks first.** Every change waits for Confirm, with the change shown first.
- **It leaves a trail.** Every action is in the audit log, marked as made by the assistant.
- **It's your choice.** Pick the AI provider and model, or run your own. It's off until an admin switches it
  on, with usage limits.

**And beyond the page.** The thread runs out of the screen and into other AI apps the team already uses.
Through MCP they connect with each person's own sign-in, read-only or read and write.

**Words.** _AI that works inside your CRM, on your terms._

**What it proves.** The assistant (questions, summaries, reports, charts, drafts, changes with
confirmation), the guardrails, provider choice, and MCP.

## Act 7: The label and the vault (ownership and trust)

**Scene.** The garment is turned inside out. Instead of a maker's label, a blank one: as the camera nears,
it stitches itself with _Harbour Dental_ and the clinic's logo. Below the garment, the mat opens into a
lit vault with one door and one name on it.

**Words.** _Your name on the label. Your data in your vault._

- Your own instance and your own database, hosted where you choose. Export everything at any time.
- Roles and record access, two-factor sign-in, an audit log nobody can edit.
- Compliance settings for privacy-sensitive work: access logging, data subject requests, retention,
  encryption of sensitive fields.
- No per-seat licence fees.

**For agencies:** the same label, sewn in for their clients. Every instance is fully white-label: the client
sees their own name, logo, colors and domain.

**What it proves.** Data ownership, security, compliance readiness, white-label.

## Act 8: Alterations (it grows with you)

**Scene.** A year later. The garment is back on the mat. The clinic has opened a second site. The tape
measure comes out again, and this time it's quick: one new location field, a new role, a new pipeline for
cosmetic treatments, a new module stitched on from the catalog. The garment is altered, not remade.

**Words.** _Tailored once. Altered whenever you need._

**What it proves.** Ongoing change without a rebuild: settings, objects, modules, integrations, an API and
webhooks.

## Finale: Book your fitting

**Scene.** The camera rises above the mat. All nine garments stand in a quiet row, each a different shape,
all made from the same cloth. The gold tape comes to rest under the final line.

**Words.** _Your CRM, cut to measure._ **Book a fitting.** _Or try the live demo._

Under it, the line already on the site: built and supported by a company with more than 24 years of
business software behind it.

## Tone and guardrails for the page

- Calm, crafted, confident: a tailor's workshop at night, not a sci-fi spaceship. Motion is slow and
  deliberate; the gold accent stays rare.
- Every claim matches the product as it is today. Numbers marked as placeholders on the current site
  (time to go live, testimonials) stay placeholders.
- Harbour Dental and Dr. Anika Rao are fictional and should be labelled as an example.
- The story must work without 3D: with reduced motion or on a slow phone, each act becomes a still image,
  its words and its product screen.
